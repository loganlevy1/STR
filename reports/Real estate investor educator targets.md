# Mapping Cliffco's Best Educator Partnerships

Roughly **70 small-to-medium real estate investing educators, coaches, and influencer-led brands** across the STR/Airbnb, BRRRR/buy-and-hold/DSCR, fix-and-flip/wholesaling, and multifamily/development niches sit in the "Michael Chang tier" — audiences in the thousands to low hundreds of thousands, monetized through $2,000–$50,000 courses/masterminds, and structurally primed to refer their students to a preferred lender. The clearest, best-documented partnership mechanism already exists: DSCR and hard-money lenders such as Kiavi, OfferMarket, and Total Quality Lending run formal affiliate programs paying **$1,000 per closed loan** for referrals from exactly this audience ([Kiavi Affiliate Program](https://www.kiavi.com/affiliates)), proving the model Cliffco would be entering is proven, not speculative. Audience-size verification is the single biggest gap in this research — most follower/subscriber counts could not be confirmed live due to tool access limits and must be manually re-checked before outreach — but sub-niche fit, monetization model, and contact channels are well-documented for nearly every name below. A handful of prospects carry serious reputational risk (fraud litigation, FTC action, BBB complaints) and are flagged for exclusion. The strongest immediate opportunities cluster around educators who already run "built-in funding partner" language in their programs (e.g., Bill Allen/7 Figure Flipping), regional REIA chapters that are functionally lender-sponsored already, and the fast-growing Skool-hosted community layer, which is cheaper and lower-friction to enter than national conferences like BiggerPockets' BPCON or the NPLA Conference.

## Reputational and compliance flags — exclude or diligence-first

Three names in this roster carry documented risk that should keep them off an active outreach list, and two more warrant a compliance check before any conversation begins. **Clayton Morris/Morris Invest** faces 2019 lawsuits in Indiana and New Jersey alleging a Ponzi-like scheme built on selling unrehabbed or non-existent turnkey properties ([InvestFourMore](https://investfourmore.com/clayton-morris-morris-invest-fraud-accusations/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton_Morris)) — this is a hard exclusion regardless of his audience size. **Tarek El Moussa and Christina Hall**, while verified as real, active educators behind "Real Estate Elevated," were separately paid promotional faces for Zurixx LLC's training seminars, which the FTC sanctioned for deceptive marketing, resulting in more than $12 million in consumer refunds ([Darien Times/AP](https://www.darientimes.com/realestate/article/victims-real-estate-scheme-involving-hgtv-s-19652705.php)); combined with their mainstream TV-celebrity scale, they are not a realistic small-medium partnership target. **The Multifamily Mindset** (Tyler Deveraux and Ryan Wooley) has an open Better Business Bureau profile and third-party complaints alleging coaches committed to sponsoring student deals and then withdrew, causing real financial losses ([BBB listing](https://www.bbb.org/us/fl/ocoee/profile/wealth-building-seminars/the-multi-family-mindset-0733-90828368); [ScamRisk](https://www.scamrisk.com/multi-family-mindset-review/)) — exclude or require significant extra diligence. **Brad Sumrok's** CRE Investor Coach program was the subject of a 2023 investigative feature in The Real Deal documenting cracks in his coaching empire and member complaints ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/magazine/national-october-2023/multifamily-mentor-brad-sumrok-built-an-empire-now-the-cracks-are-showing/)) — treat as a caution flag requiring manual diligence, not an outright exclusion. Finally, **David Greene** runs his own mortgage brokerage ("The One Brokerage") alongside his BiggerPockets-tied media presence, which makes him a potential channel competitor rather than a clean partnership prospect and should be evaluated with that conflict in mind before any outreach.

## STR/Airbnb educators split into scaling, arbitrage, and design niches

The STR education space cleanly separates into three sub-segments with different financing relevance: general acquisition/scaling educators (**Rob Abasolo/Robuilt-Host Camp**, **Michael Elefante/BNB Investor Academy**, **Tony J. & Sara Robinson/Alpha Host**, **Michael Sjogren/Short Term Rental Secrets**), rental-arbitrage specialists (**10XBNB**, **"Coach Cav"/YourSpaceSTR**, **Airbnb Breakdown Academy**), and hospitality/design-forward specialists (**Sarah Karakaian & Annette Grant/Thanks For Visiting**, **Justin Miller/Showplace**). This split matters directly for loan-product fit: arbitrage-model students lease rather than buy, so they are largely irrelevant to a DSCR or bridge-loan pipeline, while acquisition/scaling and design-forward audiences are buying and renovating property and need exactly the products Cliffco underwrites. **Avery Carl's The Short Term Shop** stands out as the most verifiable large-audience STR target — a specialist brokerage with **250+ personally-owned doors** and a client base of **5,000+ STR investors** across roughly 20 markets, reaching ~97K Instagram followers on its business account ([Grit Daily](https://gritdaily.com/the-short-term-shop-mastering-short-term-rentals/); [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/theshorttermshop/)). **Sean Rakidzich** (Airbnb Automated/Cracking Superhost/Revande) has the largest confirmed YouTube following in the niche at **322,000+ subscribers** ([Hostaway](https://www.hostaway.com/blog/sean-rakidzich-airbnb-automated/)), though much of his audience runs an arbitrage model, tempering direct mortgage relevance. Course pricing across the acquisition-focused STR educators clusters at **$2,000–$12,000** (BNB Mastery ~$2K, Host Camp ~$7K, BNB Investor Academy ~$10–12K), a useful proxy for audience capital availability. Skool has emerged as a common community-hosting platform across at least four STR educators in this roster, making it a practical discovery channel for finding additional smaller targets beyond this list.

## BRRRR, buy-and-hold, and DSCR educators reveal a content gap Cliffco could fill

No individual educator in this niche has built a brand narrowly around "DSCR loan portfolio scaling" as a headline topic — DSCR education online is presently dominated by lenders' own content (Visio Lending, LendingOne, Dominion Financial) rather than by a dedicated creator brand, which is either a genuine market white space or a co-creation opportunity for Cliffco to sponsor DSCR-specific content with an existing BRRRR educator. Among verified individuals, **Chad "Coach" Carson** anchors the space with roughly 140,000 YouTube subscribers (a figure from a milestone post, not independently re-confirmed) and a brand built explicitly around "small and mighty" buy-and-hold investing rather than institutional scale ([Coach Carson](https://www.coachcarson.com/)). **Sam Primm/Faster Freedom** shows the largest raw reach in the group — 623K Instagram, ~94K YouTube, and 1.7M TikTok followers per third-party sources — but those numbers are unverified and, if confirmed, may push him past the "small-medium" ceiling the assignment targets ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/samfasterfreedom/)). **Craig Curelop's House Hacking University** and **Eric Hughes/Rental Income Advisors** (100+ coached clients purchasing **$30M+ in rental properties**) represent the cohort-course and 1:1-coaching monetization archetypes, respectively, both of which already function as lead-gen funnels that regularly refer clients to lenders as part of the coaching process ([Rental Income Advisors](https://www.rentalincomeadvisors.com/rental-property-coaching)). Several credible names — **Ashley Kehr**, **David Greene**, **Whitney Elkins-Hutten** — sit in a hybrid zone with meaningful BiggerPockets or institutional-syndication ties and should be tagged accordingly rather than treated as fully independent small creators.

## Fix-and-flip and wholesaling educators are the most financing-ready audience

This niche is where an existing lender-affiliate precedent already lives inside an educator's own marketing: **Bill Allen's 7 Figure Flipping** mastermind explicitly advertises "**100% purchase and renovation financing through our built-in funding partners**" as a program benefit ([7figureflipping.com](https://www.7figureflipping.com/)), making him the strongest direct comparable for how Cliffco could structure a co-marketing or embedded-financing arrangement. **Jerry Norton's Flipping Mastery TV** has the largest confirmed audience in this niche at **over 500,000 YouTube subscribers** and is described as the #1 wholesaling/flipping channel, with students reportedly having flipped $100M–$500M+ in real estate ([RealEstateBees](https://realestatebees.com/course/flipping-mastery/)). **Pace Morby/Subto** (300K+ Instagram) and **Jamil Damji** (197K personal Instagram, plus the KeyGlee wholesaling franchise operating in 118 markets) round out the largest-audience tier, though Morby's creative-financing focus (subject-to, seller financing) means his students are less likely to need traditional purchase-money mortgages. For financing relevance specifically, the strongest sub-niche fits are educators whose audiences actually acquire and renovate property rather than pure paper-wholesalers who never take title: **Bill Allen**, **Jerry Norton**, **Justin Colby**, **Chris Rood**, and **Sharad Mehta's** 1,400+-member wholesaler mastermind. Pure wholesaling educators (**Brent Daniels/TTP**, **Tom Krol**, **Kent Clothier**) reach an earlier-stage audience less likely to need rehab financing directly, though many wholesalers graduate into flipping and remain a relevant top-of-funnel audience. **Cody Sperber/Clever Investor**, with 150,000+ historical students and repeated Inc. 5000 recognition, likely sits closer to a mature mega-brand than a small-medium target and should be sized manually before prioritizing outreach.

## Multifamily and development educators are also active borrowers, not just influencers

The critical structural insight in this niche is that nearly every named educator runs an active capital-raising or property-acquisition business alongside their education brand — meaning outreach here is simultaneously outreach to an audience *and* to potential direct borrowers for construction, bridge, and portfolio loans on the educators' own deals. Dedicated ground-up/spec-construction education is comparatively underserved: **Ernest & Anthony Aguilar's "Ground Up Developers"** podcast is the clearest niche fit, explicitly covering new construction, fix-and-flip, long-term rental, and Airbnb development projects with GC, realtor, and architect guests ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/ground-up-developers/id1608412088)). Most other "development" content is instead bundled into value-add multifamily syndication education rather than offered as a pure-play construction-coaching brand — **Ashley Wilson (Apartment Addicts/Bar Down Investments)** has personally managed **$13M+ in construction**, making her program relevant to construction-finance conversations even though her core brand is multifamily syndication. Self-storage (**Scott Meyers/SelfStorageInvesting.com**, owning 2.4M+ sq ft and 14,000+ units) and mobile home parks (**Kevin Bupp/Mobile Home Park Academy**) are well-established adjacent alternative-asset niches whose audiences also regularly need construction and bridge financing for expansion. Several names in this niche — **Rod Khleif** (1,700+ Warrior members, 260,000 claimed student-owned units), **Joe Fairless/Ashcroft Capital** (institutional-scale AUM), **Neal Bawa** ($436M portfolio) — have likely scaled beyond the "small-medium" target tier and should be weighted with that caveat; they remain useful as reference points for program structure even if deprioritized for direct sponsorship spend.

## The affiliate-referral model is proven; flat sponsorship deals are the underused lever

The market landscape research confirms a mature, multi-layered ecosystem: large aggregator platforms (BiggerPockets), individual educator funnels on Kajabi/Teachable-style infrastructure, the fast-growing **Skool** community layer, the offline **REIA** network, and a dedicated private-lending conference circuit all overlap, with the same educator often running a free YouTube channel, a Skool community, a paid course, and a mastermind simultaneously. The best-documented lender-to-educator partnership mechanism is the **performance-based affiliate program**: **Kiavi** requires affiliates to have a network capable of referring at least 30 investors per year and pays **$1,000 per qualified closed loan** within 12 months, with borrowers separately receiving a $350 closing bonus ([Kiavi Affiliate Program](https://www.kiavi.com/affiliates)); **OfferMarket** and **Total Quality Lending** run comparable programs explicitly courting "influencers" and "creators" ([OfferMarket](https://www.offermarket.us/referral-program); [Total Quality Lending](https://www.totalqualitylending.com/partners/affiliate-partner)). **BiggerPockets' Featured Lender program** reaches "more than 2 million investors" and its Pro Perks tier already includes named lender partners Kiavi and LendingOne, illustrating the ceiling of what a fully professionalized platform partnership looks like ([BiggerPockets](https://www.biggerpockets.com/blog/lender-lead-bundles-for-investors-biggerpockets)). Notably, no publicly documented case study of a real-estate-investing *educator* (as distinct from a mortgage-industry loan-officer-influencer) disclosing a co-branded sponsored webinar with a DSCR or hard-money lender was found — the affiliate mechanism is confirmed and actively recruited for, but flat-fee sponsorship deals appear to be negotiated privately rather than advertised, representing a comparatively unclaimed channel. REIA sponsorships offer a lower-cost, lower-friction entry point than national conferences: local chapter vendor access runs **$300–$5,000**, versus tens of thousands to $100K+ for platform-level events like BPCON or the NPLA Conference, and REIA chapters are frequently organized or effectively hosted by a single sponsoring lender already, meaning the "educational association" and "lender lead-gen funnel" roles often blur at the local level ([Boston REIA Sponsorship](https://bostonrealestateinvestorsassociation.com/boston-reia-sponsorship-reach-local-real-estate-investors/)).

## Suggested next steps for outreach

Cliffco's loan officer team has at least five realistic, evidence-backed partnership mechanisms to pursue with the roster above, roughly ordered from lowest-friction to highest-commitment. **Affiliate/referral deals** are the best-proven starting point: the Kiavi/OfferMarket/Total Quality Lending model of a flat per-closed-loan bounty (commonly ~$1,000) is directly replicable and gives Cliffco a low-risk way to test individual educator relationships before committing marketing spend — start with financing-ready audiences like Bill Allen (7 Figure Flipping, which already runs a "built-in funding partners" model), Jerry Norton (Flipping Mastery), and Avery Carl (The Short Term Shop). **Sponsored webinars or co-created content** represent the most underused lever identified in this research — no named educator was found publicly disclosing an existing lender-webinar partnership, so a well-executed DSCR- or bridge-loan-focused webinar with a mid-tier BRRRR or STR educator (e.g., Coach Carson, Craig Curelop, or Bill Faeth) could be genuinely differentiated rather than "me too." **Preferred-lender placements** inside a paid community or course (the BiggerPockets Pro Perks model, scaled down) fit naturally with Skool-hosted communities and coaching programs that already refer students to vendors as part of onboarding — Sharad Mehta's 1,400-member wholesaler mastermind and Michael Sjogren's Short Term Rental Secrets mastermind are plausible early targets. **Mastermind and event sponsorships** should be pursued selectively given cost: national events (BPCON, NPLA Conference) likely command $5K–$100K+ sponsorship tiers dominated by larger, better-capitalized lenders, so Cliffco's spend is better concentrated on **regional REIA chapter sponsorships** ($300–$5,000) and smaller niche conferences (Midwest REI Conference, InvestHER CON), which offer direct investor-room access at a fraction of the cost. Across every mechanism, the team should budget time for a **manual verification pass** — visiting each target's live YouTube/Instagram/podcast channel and website contact page directly — since the majority of audience-size figures in the roster above trace to third-party estimates rather than the educators' own platforms, and a handful of names carry compliance risk (see the flags section) that must be cleared before any outreach begins.

## Conclusion

The research surfaces a market that is structurally ready for exactly the kind of partnership Cliffco is likely to propose: performance-based lender affiliate programs are already an established, actively-recruited category in this space, and a meaningful share of the profiled educators — particularly in fix-and-flip/wholesaling and BRRRR — already route their students toward outside financing partners as a normal part of their coaching funnel. The more surprising finding is where the market is thin rather than crowded: there is no dedicated "DSCR-only" educator brand and only one clear ground-up-construction specialist (Ground Up Developers), which means Cliffco's most differentiated move may not be competing for an existing educator's affiliate slot but co-creating content in an underserved sub-niche where no incumbent lender has yet built a relationship. Given how much of this roster's audience-size and pricing data rests on third-party estimates rather than confirmed platform data, the highest-leverage next step before committing marketing dollars is a focused manual-verification sprint on the dozen or so names flagged "confirmed" or near-confirmed in the table above, since those are the targets whose scale and fit can be trusted without further diligence.
