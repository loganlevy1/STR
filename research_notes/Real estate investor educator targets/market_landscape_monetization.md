# Real Estate Investment Education Market: Landscape & Lender/Vendor Monetization Patterns

## What does the real estate investment education market look like structurally in 2026?

### Takeaway
The market is a layered ecosystem rather than a single business type: (1) large aggregator platforms/communities (BiggerPockets being the dominant example), (2) individual educator "funnels" built on course platforms (Kajabi/Teachable-style infrastructure) feeding paid coaching and masterminds, (3) paid membership communities increasingly hosted on Skool, (4) a long-standing offline layer of Real Estate Investor Associations (REIAs) and local meetups, and (5) a dedicated conference/event circuit (private-lending and REI-focused) where sponsorship and lead-gen happen face-to-face. These layers overlap — the same educator often runs a YouTube/podcast channel, a free Skool or Facebook community, a paid Kajabi-hosted course, and a mastermind, while also appearing at REIA meetings and investor conferences.

### Cited Findings
- National REIA is a 501(c)(6) trade association founded in 1985, describing itself as a federation of local associations/investment clubs, with 120+ local chapters/affiliate groups and roughly 40,000 individual members nationally. — [National REIA](https://nationalreia.org/) (via search snippet; direct fetch blocked by network egress proxy)
- REIA local chapter meetings are frequently organized or sponsored by a company (e.g., a hard money lender), and that sponsor's presentations often center on how their product (e.g., hard money financing) can help members' deals; networking with sponsors/vendors typically happens before the formal meeting starts. — [RetipsterREIA definition page](https://retipster.com/terms/reia/) (per search snippet; direct fetch blocked)
- Vendor/sponsor participation at REIA meetings has real cost figures: some meetings require vendors to pay $1,000–$5,000 for a member package/access, while other REIA sponsorship packages (e.g., New York REIA) start as low as $300. — [RetipsterREIA definition page](https://retipster.com/terms/reia/) (per search snippet)
- Boston REIA runs a dedicated sponsorship program explicitly pitched as a way to "reach local real estate investors." — [Boston REIA Sponsorship](https://bostonrealestateinvestorsassociation.com/boston-reia-sponsorship-reach-local-real-estate-investors/)
- Skool has become a hosting platform for real estate investing communities that bundle a community feed with full paid or free courses; examples include "Real Estate Skool" (led by David Dodge and Mike Slane, ~563 members, free to join, offering courses on wholesaling/flipping, private money borrowing, landlording/rentals, and seller financing/"slow flips"), "The Real Estate Investing Club" (158 members, free or $49/month premium tier for weekly group calls), and other smaller communities like "Property Investing Community" and "Real Estate Investment Club." — [Skool: Real Estate Skool](https://www.skool.com/realestate/about); [Discount Property Investor roundup](https://www.discountpropertyinvestor.com/blog/best-free-real-estate-investing-community-on-skool-in-2026)
- BiggerPockets is the dominant large-scale aggregator in the space: free-to-join core platform with a paid "Pro" tier (LinkedIn-style freemium upgrade model), plus revenue from affiliate partnerships and advertising; historical reporting describes it as reaching a community of 256,000+ members with 5 million monthly visits (note: this figure is from an older interview and BiggerPockets' current scale is likely larger — its own advertising materials describe reaching "more than 2 million investors," which may reflect a different/updated audience metric). — [LevelingUp/Joshua Dorkin interview](https://www.levelingup.com/growth-everywhere-interview/joshua-dorkin-biggerpockets/); [BiggerPockets lender lead bundles blog](https://www.biggerpockets.com/blog/lender-lead-bundles-for-investors-biggerpockets)
- Dedicated private-lending/hard-money industry conferences exist as a distinct event layer: the NPLA (National Private Lenders Association) Conference (held March 15–17, 2026 at the Loews Miami Beach Hotel) is described as one of the largest gatherings for private lending/nonbank real estate finance professionals, featuring a sponsor hall where lenders and service providers showcase solutions; the National Private Lender Expo (May 2025, St. Petersburg, FL) connects private lenders, investors, and high-net-worth individuals. — [NPLA Conference](https://nplaconference.com/); [Easy Street Capital NPLA page](https://easystreetcap.com/conferences/npla-conference-2026/); [National Private Lender Expo](https://www.easystreetcap.com/conferences/private-lender-expo/)
- BiggerPockets itself runs an annual flagship conference (BPCON, e.g., BPCON2026 in Orlando) with its own sponsorship packet for vendors/lenders. — [BPCON2026 Orlando](https://get.biggerpockets.com/bpcon-2026/)
- Regional/niche REI conferences with formal sponsor programs include the Midwest REI Conference (MREIC) and InvestHER CON (a women-in-real-estate-investing conference with a public sponsors page). — [MREIC Sponsorship](https://www.midwestreiconference.com/sponsorship-opportunities); [InvestHER CON Sponsors](https://therealestateinvesther.com/sponsors)

### Inferences
- The REIA/local-chapter layer functions as a grassroots, lower-cost analog to the national conference circuit — sponsorship costs there ($300–$5,000) are an order of magnitude below national conference sponsorships ($5,000–$100,000+, see below), making REIAs a more accessible entry point for small/regional lenders and hard money shops to reach investors directly.
- Because REIA chapters are frequently organized or effectively "hosted" by a sponsoring business (e.g., a lender), the line between "educational association" and "lender-run lead-gen funnel" is often blurry at the local level.
- BiggerPockets acts as a structural bridge between the "guru/educator" economy and institutional lenders — it packages audience access into named commercial products (Pro Perks, Featured Lender program) rather than leaving it to ad-hoc influencer deals, suggesting it is more professionalized/monetized than most individual educators.

## What is the typical business model / funnel for a small-to-medium real estate investing educator?

### Takeaway
The consistently described model is a "value ladder" / upsell funnel: free content (YouTube, podcast, social media, free community) → lead magnet or free webinar/seminar → low-ticket course → higher-ticket coaching → invite-only mastermind or paid community, with masterminds/coaching often priced far higher than the entry course.

### Cited Findings
- The typical "guru" funnel starts with a free seminar or webinar promising easy wealth, then sells a basic course, followed by an expensive coaching program, and finally exclusive investment opportunities or advanced training at even higher price points; once a buyer makes an initial purchase they enter an "up-sell funnel" that can escalate to coaching programs costing $40,000 or more. — [Start With Cents: Real Estate Guru Scams Exposed](https://www.startwithcents.org/posts/real-estate-guru-scams-exposed)
- Paid mastermind/event pricing in the space commonly ranges from $500–$25,000+ for events, with premium annual masterminds running $10,000–$50,000+. — [Rod Khleif: Top Real Estate Investing Communities 2026](https://rodkhleif.com/top-real-estate-investing-communities-networks-to-join-in-2026/)
- Lower-tier paid communities exist well below mastermind pricing — e.g., a "Buy It, Rent It, Profit" membership platform priced at $650 for unlimited access to resources/tools/education, and Skool-hosted communities charging as little as $49/month for premium tiers. — [Buy It, Rent It, Profit](https://buyitrentitprofit.com/product/mastermind/); [Discount Property Investor Skool roundup](https://www.discountpropertyinvestor.com/blog/best-free-real-estate-investing-community-on-skool-in-2026)
- Evaluation criteria commonly cited for judging these paid communities/masterminds include: how many members closed deals in the last 12 months, whether results are documented/verifiable, the coaching-to-member ratio, how deal review works, refund/guarantee policy, and ability to speak with current members before joining — implying these are the standard due-diligence questions prospective buyers are told to ask, which in turn implies these are also the standard trust/credibility levers educators must market against. — [Rod Khleif: Masterminds & Bootcamps 2026](https://rodkhleif.com/best-real-estate-masterminds-and-bootcamps-2025-edition/)

### Inferences
- The funnel structure (free → low-ticket → high-ticket coaching → mastermind) is the same underlying mechanic used across the wider "course creator"/info-product economy, applied to real estate; this makes real estate investing education structurally similar to other online-guru niches (trading, e-commerce, dropshipping) rather than uniquely real-estate-specific.
- Because masterminds/coaching (not the underlying real estate deals) appear to be the primary revenue driver described in critical sources, an educator's incentive to monetize an audience via lender/vendor partnerships (affiliate fees, sponsorships) is likely an attractive *additional*, lower-effort revenue stream layered on top of the course/coaching funnel — sponsorship revenue doesn't require producing new course content or 1:1 coaching capacity.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative source with a full breakdown of revenue mix (e.g., "X% of revenue from courses vs. coaching vs. sponsorships") for a typical small/medium educator was found; this figure likely doesn't exist publicly and would need to be inferred or estimated qualitatively in the final report.
- The "guru scam" framing sources are critical/skeptical in tone (Start With Cents, Andreas Mueller Substack); their claims about $40k+ coaching pricing and "most gurus have never flipped a house" are useful for describing perception/criticism of the market but should be flagged as opinionated/critical commentary rather than neutral industry data.

## How big is "small to medium" typically in this space?

### Takeaway
No standardized industry definition of "small to medium" real estate investing educator was found; however, cross-referencing general creator-economy income/CPM data with real estate investing community member counts suggests a rough working band of low tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand across audience channels (e.g., 10K–200K YouTube subscribers, low-hundreds to low-thousands of paid community members) as distinguishing an educator with a real, monetizable audience from both hobbyist-scale creators and celebrity-scale gurus.

### Cited Findings
- General creator-economy benchmarks (not real-estate-specific): "micro-influencers" are defined inconsistently across sources as either 1,000–10,000 followers, or alternatively 10,000–100,000 followers with "niche-focused, strong community trust" positioning. — [Ubiquitous: Micro-Influencer Marketing 2026 Update](https://www.ubiquitousinfluence.com/post/micro-influencers); [Afluencer: Micro-Influencer Marketing for Real Estate](https://afluencer.com/micro-influencer-marketing-for-real-estate/)
- Real estate micro-influencers are reported to typically charge $250–$2,000 per sponsored post, well below what larger, broad-audience influencers command. — [LaunchpointHQ: Real Estate Micro-Influencer Rate Guide 2026](https://www.launchpointhq.com/guides/rates/how-much-do-real-estate-micro-influencers-charge)
- On YouTube specifically, a creator needs roughly 100K+ subscribers in a mid-RPM niche or 50K+ subscribers in a high-RPM niche (education content is cited as a high-paying niche at $5–$18 CPM) to build a sustainable, diversified income when combined with 2–3 additional revenue streams (which for real estate would include courses/coaching/sponsorships). — [Montage: How Much Do YouTubers Make 2026](https://montage.app/blog/how-much-do-youtubers-make); [UxerWave: How Much YouTubers Make](https://uxerwave.com/monetization/how-much-youtubers-make/)
- Observed real, named-example community sizes in the real estate investing Skool space (illustrative of "small-medium" scale communities, not celebrity scale) include 563 members (Real Estate Skool) and 158 members (The Real Estate Investing Club) — both far smaller than BiggerPockets' 200K+/multi-million-visit scale. — [Skool: Real Estate Skool](https://www.skool.com/realestate/about); [Skool: The Real Estate Investing Club](https://www.skool.com/the-real-estate-investing-club/about)

### Inferences
- Given (a) the ~50K–100K+ YouTube subscriber threshold associated with sustainable multi-stream creator income, and (b) real observed RE investing Skool communities in the low hundreds of paid/free members, a reasonable synthesis band for "small-to-medium" real estate investing educators (big enough to be a viable sponsorship/partnership target, not yet celebrity-scale) is roughly: 10K–250K YouTube subscribers or Instagram/TikTok followers, an email list in the low thousands to tens of thousands, and a paid community/course customer base in the hundreds to low thousands. This is an inference synthesized from general creator-economy data and specific but limited real-estate examples — it is not a figure stated directly by any single source and should be treated as a reasoned estimate, not a hard citation.

### Gaps
- No real-estate-specific study or industry report was found that explicitly segments "small," "medium," and "large/celebrity" real estate investing educators by audience size or revenue. This is a genuine data gap; the band above is inferred, not sourced from a single authority, and the report writer should treat it as an estimate.
- No data found on typical email list sizes specifically for real estate investing educators.

## How do these educators typically monetize sponsorships/partnerships with outside vendors (lenders, etc.)?

### Takeaway
The clearest, best-documented mechanism is direct affiliate/referral programs run by DSCR and hard-money/fix-and-flip lenders that explicitly recruit "influencers, content creators, and community leaders" and pay flat cash bounties per closed loan referral; beyond affiliate programs, educators and aggregator platforms also monetize via sponsored webinars/events, "featured lender" or "preferred lender" placements, paid conference sponsorships, and (for large platforms like BiggerPockets) formal Pro Perks/partner programs.

### Cited Findings
- Kiavi (a major DSCR/fix-and-flip lender) runs a formal affiliate program explicitly targeting "experts, leaders, creators, influencers, and advocates" in residential real estate investing; affiliates must have a network capable of referring at least 30 real estate investors per year; commission is $1,000 per qualified loan a referral closes within 12 months, plus limited-time double-payout bonus promotions for top performers; referred borrowers separately receive $350 cash after closing their first qualified loan. Mortgage brokers, real estate agents, and companies with existing Kiavi partnership agreements are excluded from the affiliate program. Signup/tracking runs through PartnerStack. — [Kiavi Affiliate Program](https://www.kiavi.com/affiliates)
- OfferMarket (a DSCR/fix-and-flip/slow-flip lender) explicitly courts "real estate investors, wholesalers, influencers, and other real estate service providers" for its referral program, paying via shared referral links. — [OfferMarket Referral Program](https://www.offermarket.us/referral-program)
- Total Quality Lending runs an affiliate program explicitly aimed at "real estate tech companies, platforms, and influencers," describing itself as connecting these audiences with a lending partner. — [Total Quality Lending Affiliate Partner](https://www.totalqualitylending.com/partners/affiliate-partner)
- BiggerPockets operates a "Featured Lender" program allowing lenders to reach "more than 2 million investors" on the platform and get matched with investors seeking financing; separately, BiggerPockets' "Pro Perks" membership program includes named lender partnerships (Kiavi and LendingOne) plus landlord-insurance company Steadily, giving Pro members institutional-level pricing/access. — [BiggerPockets Lender Lead Bundles blog](https://www.biggerpockets.com/blog/lender-lead-bundles-for-investors-biggerpockets); [BiggerPockets Pro Perks / citybiz coverage](https://www.citybiz.co/article/813208/biggerpockets-unlocks-institutional-grade-pricing-power-for-everyday-investors-with-pro-perks-membership-expansion/)
- General real estate affiliate program benchmarks exist beyond lending specifically: BiggerPockets itself pays $75 per Pro membership referred, plus $40 per real estate lead and $40 per lender lead generated by its own affiliates; Buildium (property management software) pays 25% commission per monthly subscription; DealCheck (property analysis software) pays 30% recurring commission with bonus payouts every 10 signups. — [ButterflyMX: Top 11 Real Estate Affiliate Programs](https://butterflymx.com/blog/real-estate-affiliate-programs/); [NewSilver: Top 9 Highest Paying Real Estate Affiliate Programs](https://newsilver.com/the-lender/real-estate-affiliate-programs/)
- REIA local chapter meetings are frequently organized/sponsored directly by a lender (e.g., a hard money lender), with the meeting content itself built around that lender's product; vendor tables/sponsor access at these meetings costs roughly $300–$5,000 depending on the chapter and package. — [Boston REIA Sponsorship page](https://bostonrealestateinvestorsassociation.com/boston-reia-sponsorship-reach-local-real-estate-investors/); Retipster REIA page (per search snippet; direct fetch blocked)
- Mortgage/lending companies are also increasingly building their own influencer-facing divisions and direct-hire partnerships rather than only running affiliate programs: NFM Lending created a dedicated "Influencer Division" (led by a person described as the "#1 TikTok creator in mortgage") to build training/content platforms for loan officers and agents; UMortgage brought on YouTube creator "Matt The Mortgage Guy" (Matt Gougé) as part of its corporate brokerage portfolio; brokerage West Capital Lending partnered in 2023 with the 15-member "What's A Mortgage" (WAM) loan-officer-influencer team as an experimental lead-generation channel. — [NFM Lending Influencer Division](https://nfmlending.com/influencer-division/); [National Mortgage Professional: Mortgage Industry Now Recruiting YouTube Influencers](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/mortgage-industry-now-recruiting-youtube-influencers); [National Mortgage Professional: The Rise of Mortgage Influencers](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/rise-mortgage-influencers)
- Note on scope: these NFM/UMortgage/WAM examples are lenders partnering with mortgage-industry (loan officer/consumer-mortgage) influencers rather than real-estate-*investing* educators specifically — relevant as a proof-of-concept that the lending industry actively builds influencer partnership infrastructure, but not a direct example of an investment-education educator partnership.

### Inferences
- The DSCR/hard-money lending segment (Kiavi, OfferMarket, Total Quality Lending, LendingOne) is the most active and formalized about influencer/affiliate partnerships specifically with real estate investing content creators — more so than traditional consumer mortgage lenders, which more often partner with mortgage-industry (loan officer) influencers rather than investor-education influencers. This makes sense structurally: DSCR/hard-money loans are investor-only products, so their natural affiliate audience *is* the real estate investing education/influencer audience.
- The consistent $1,000-per-closed-loan or 25–30%-recurring-commission structures suggest that lender-to-educator partnerships in this space are overwhelmingly performance-based (pay-per-conversion) rather than flat sponsorship-fee arrangements — flat-fee "sponsored webinar slot" deals likely exist too but were not as well documented in available sources, suggesting they may be negotiated privately/informally rather than advertised publicly as structured programs.
- Given REIA chapters are often directly run or funded by a single sponsoring lender, "preferred lender" relationships at the local/regional level may functionally BE the REIA's business model in many chapters, rather than being a bolt-on sponsorship to an independently-run association.

### Gaps
- Could not find a specific, named, verifiable case study of a *real estate investing educator/influencer* (as opposed to a mortgage-industry loan-officer-influencer) publicly disclosing a "our lender partner" sponsored webinar or branded co-marketing campaign with a DSCR or hard-money lender. The Kiavi/OfferMarket/Total Quality Lending affiliate programs confirm the *mechanism* exists and is actively recruited for, but a concrete named example (e.g., "Educator X partnered with Lender Y for a co-branded webinar") was not found in this research pass and should be flagged as a gap — the report writer or other researchers covering named individuals may be better positioned to surface a specific instance.

- Could not verify BiggerPockets' current advertising rate card or podcast sponsorship pricing directly (biggerpockets.com was blocked by network egress proxy); only secondary confirmation that AdvertiseCast/Libsyn holds an exclusive "seven-figure deal" as the sales channel partner for BiggerPockets podcast ad inventory was found. — [Podnews: AdvertiseCast/BiggerPockets partnership](https://podnews.net/press-release/advertisecast-biggerpockets)

## What are relevant REIAs, conferences, and community platforms where lender partnerships/sponsorships commonly happen?

### Takeaway
Lender-educator/investor contact points cluster into four venue types: (1) National REIA and its 120+ local chapters, (2) dedicated national private-lending conferences (NPLA Conference, National Private Lender Expo), (3) large investor-education platform conferences (BiggerPockets' BPCON) and regional/niche REI conferences (Midwest REI Conference, InvestHER CON), and (4) online communities, increasingly hosted on Skool alongside the older BiggerPockets forums.

### Cited Findings
- National REIA: 501(c)(6) national trade association, 120+ local chapters/affiliate groups, ~40,000 members; serves as an umbrella over local investor associations, property owner associations, apartment associations, and landlord associations. — [National REIA](https://nationalreia.org/) (per search snippet)
- NPLA Conference: major private-lending/nonbank real estate finance industry conference, March 15–17 2026, Loews Miami Beach Hotel, includes a sponsor hall for lenders/service providers. — [NPLA Conference site](https://nplaconference.com/); [Easy Street Capital](https://easystreetcap.com/conferences/npla-conference-2026/)
- National Private Lender Expo: connects private lenders, real estate investors, and high-net-worth individuals; held May 2025 in St. Petersburg, FL. — [Easy Street Capital](https://www.easystreetcap.com/conferences/private-lender-expo/)
- BiggerPockets BPCON2026 (Orlando): the platform's flagship annual conference with its own sponsorship packet for vendors. — [BPCON2026](https://get.biggerpockets.com/bpcon-2026/)
- Midwest REI Conference (MREIC 2027) and InvestHER CON (women-in-real-estate-investing conference) both maintain public sponsorship-opportunity pages aimed at vendors/lenders wanting booth space and attendee access. — [MREIC](https://www.midwestreiconference.com/sponsorship-opportunities); [InvestHER CON](https://therealestateinvesther.com/sponsors)
- Boston REIA and New York REIA both maintain formal, publicly listed sponsorship programs for local businesses (including presumably lenders) to reach their investor member base, with NY REIA packages starting around $300. — [Boston REIA](https://bostonrealestateinvestorsassociation.com/boston-reia-sponsorship-reach-local-real-estate-investors/); Retipster (per search snippet)
- Skool hosts numerous real estate investing communities that combine community discussion with paid/free course content, functioning as a newer, lower-overhead alternative to REIAs/forums for educators to build and monetize an audience (e.g., Real Estate Skool, The Real Estate Investing Club, Property Investing Community, Real Estate Investment Club, Multifamily Money Makers). — [Skool search results, multiple community pages]

### Inferences
- The conference/event sponsorship market is clearly stratified by scale: national platform-level events (BPCON, NPLA) command sponsorship in the tens of thousands to $100K+ range based on general industry sponsorship-tier patterns (platinum ~$100K, gold ~$50K, bronze $5–10K per general conference sponsorship sources), while local REIA chapter sponsorships are two orders of magnitude cheaper ($300–$5,000) — meaning small/medium lenders or regional DSCR/hard-money shops likely concentrate spend at the REIA/regional-conference level rather than national flagship events, which are more likely dominated by large, well-capitalized lenders.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm exact current sponsorship tier pricing for BPCON or NPLA specifically (only general conference sponsorship-tier ranges from an unrelated general source were found); the $100K/$50K/$5-10K figures cited above come from a general "conference budget strategies" article, not from BPCON/NPLA's own sponsorship packet, so they should be treated as illustrative industry-general figures, not confirmed prices for these specific events.
- Circle.so as a community platform (mentioned in the assignment's suggested scope) did not surface any specific real estate investing community examples in this research pass — Skool appears to be the more visible/documented platform for this niche currently, but Circle-hosted RE investing communities likely also exist and were not verified.

## Are there directories, platforms, or services that list real estate investing influencers/educators for partnership or sponsorship purposes?

### Takeaway
Yes — general influencer-marketing platforms and databases (not real-estate-investing-specific, but usable for the niche) exist and are actively marketed toward real estate brands, alongside real-estate-specific "affiliate program roundup" content and BiggerPockets' own advertising/lender-partner intake channel.

### Cited Findings
- General influencer-marketing/discovery platforms marketed toward real estate brands include Influencity (psychographic/affinity/behavioral audience targeting), HypeAuditor (fake-follower detection, positioned as important for real estate due to trust/local-authenticity concerns), and Archive (creator-marketing platform that auto-captures user-generated content mentioning a brand across Instagram/TikTok/YouTube). — [Archive: Marketing Platforms for Real Estate Brands](https://archive.com/blog/marketing-platforms-real-estate-brands)
- Influencer marketing agencies specifically pitching real estate brand/developer/broker clients include Amra & Elma, described as a go-to agency for real estate developers, brokers, and property platforms for high-end visual campaigns on Instagram/YouTube/TikTok. — [Amra & Elma: Influencer Marketing Agencies for Real Estate](https://www.amraandelma.com/influencer-marketing-agencies-for-real-estate/)
- "Top real estate influencer" ranking/directory-style content exists (e.g., Favikon's "Top 20 Real Estate Influencers in the US 2026," Amra & Elma's "100 Top Real Estate Influencers") but these appear oriented toward luxury real estate brokerage/agent influencers rather than the investment-education "guru" niche specifically. — [Favikon Top 20](https://www.favikon.com/blog/top-real-estate-influencers-united-states-social-media); [Amra & Elma 100 Top](https://www.amraandelma.com/100-top-real-estate-influencers/)
- BiggerPockets' own advertising page and Featured Lender program function as a de facto directory/intake mechanism: lenders/vendors apply to advertise or become a featured/partner lender to reach the platform's investor audience, rather than there being a fully independent third-party directory purpose-built for "real estate investing educators" as sponsorship targets. — [BiggerPockets Advertise](https://www.biggerpockets.com/advertise) (per search snippet; direct fetch blocked); [BiggerPockets Lender Lead Bundles](https://www.biggerpockets.com/blog/lender-lead-bundles-for-investors-biggerpockets)
- Lender-run affiliate programs (Kiavi, OfferMarket, Total Quality Lending) themselves function as informal "directories" in the sense that they publicly solicit applications from influencers/creators/community leaders, effectively creating a self-selecting pipeline of RE investing educators seeking lender partnerships. — [Kiavi Affiliates](https://www.kiavi.com/affiliates); [OfferMarket Referral Program](https://www.offermarket.us/referral-program)

### Inferences
- There does not appear to be a mature, dedicated third-party directory or marketplace purpose-built specifically for "real estate investment education/guru" sponsorship matching (distinct from general influencer marketing platforms or luxury-agent-focused influencer rankings). This is a notable structural gap in the market: partnerships in this niche appear to form primarily through (a) lenders' own affiliate program recruitment pages, (b) direct outreach/relationship building at REIA meetings and conferences, and (c) BiggerPockets acting as the closest thing to a centralized intake point — rather than through a neutral third-party matchmaking directory.

### Gaps
- No dedicated real-estate-investing-education-specific sponsorship directory or marketplace platform was identified in this research pass; it's possible one exists but did not surface in searches, or the market may genuinely lack this kind of centralized matchmaking infrastructure, relying instead on direct/informal relationship-building. This should be flagged as an open question for the final report rather than stated as a confirmed absence.
